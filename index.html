02 / The opportunity

Business is already part of the national conversation. The people behind it are not yet a weekly destination.

Al Jadeed already has a strong culture of dialogue across politics, society and current affairs. The opportunity is to create a recognizable interview property dedicated to the people building companies, capital, industries and careers.

The whitespace

Not a market show. Not a corporate profile. A human business story.

The format uses business as the route into ambition, pressure, family, risk, identity and resilience — themes that travel well beyond a specialist business audience.

01

Recognizable ambition

Lebanese viewers know the brands and names. The show reveals what happened behind them.

02

Lebanon-to-region relevance

Many Lebanese founders and executives build across the GCC and global markets, naturally expanding the guest universe.

03

Digital-native moments

Decisions, failures, numbers, lessons and strong opinions create short-form hooks without forcing the conversation.

Current-programming signal

Al Jadeed’s official 2026 catalogue prominently features political, social and general dialogue programs. The proposed format adds a dedicated business-leader lane rather than duplicating those shows.

03 / Program concept

A conversation about what it really took.

Each episode starts with a public success story and works backward to find the private decisions, trade-offs and turning points that made it possible.

THE PERSON

Before the title

Family, upbringing, first job, first ambition, early influences and the moment they decided to build.

THE BUILD

How it happened

Capital, partners, customers, early mistakes, difficult years, breakthroughs and the operating choices behind growth.

THE PRESSURE

What nearly broke

Failures, conflict, cash pressure, crisis, reputation, leadership mistakes and decisions with real consequences.

THE NEXT MOVE

Where it goes now

Lebanon, GCC expansion, technology, succession, investment, ambition and the opportunity they see before others do.

Editorial rule

Every answer should reveal a story, a decision or a lesson.

If a question can be answered like a press release, it is not strong enough for the program.

04 / Editorial positioning

Intimate enough to feel personal. Sharp enough to matter.

01

Story, not résumé

Skip the chronology viewers can search. Find the scenes, choices and contradictions that explain the person.

02

Direct, not aggressive

Difficult questions are calm, researched and specific. Tension comes from substance, not performance.

03

Money without spectacle

Discuss funding, valuation, risk, wealth and investment where they reveal how the guest thinks — not as clickbait.

04

Lebanon without cliché

Ask concretely how infrastructure, talent, currency, regulation, instability and diaspora networks affect decisions.

05

Useful without becoming a class

Extract lessons through stories. The audience should learn without feeling they are watching a lecture.

06

Premium without being distant

Elegant production, plain-language questions and emotionally accessible storytelling keep the show broad.

Traditional corporate interviewTHE BUILDERSCelebrity confessional

Positioned between business rigor and human intimacy.

07 / Episode architecture

A clear arc. A conversation that still feels alive.

The structure protects pace and depth without turning the interview into a rigid questionnaire.

00–04
OPEN / THE HOOK

Start with the question nobody expects first.

A defining decision, a contradiction or a short cold-open quote previews the tension of the episode.

04–14
ORIGIN

Before the business.

Family, first money memory, early work, identity, ambition and the first moment the guest believed they could build something.

14–28
THE BUILD

From idea to momentum.

First customer, first hire, capital, partner choices, breakthrough, business model and the decision that changed the trajectory.

28–40
THE HARD PART

Pressure creates the best television.

Failure, crisis, money, leadership mistakes, conflict, Lebanon-specific constraints and the call they would make differently today.

40–50
THE MINDSET

How they decide now.

Investment thinking, leadership, hiring, personal habits, succession, family and what they refuse to compromise.

50–55
THE NEXT MOVE

End forward, not backward.

The industry ahead, Lebanon, the GCC, one unfinished ambition and one message to the next generation.

Timing is a proposed editorial framework. Final broadcast duration and break structure should be adapted to Al Jadeed’s schedule.

08 / Signature segments

Recurring devices that become part of the brand.

01THE FIRST

First salary. First customer. First big mistake.

Fast, specific questions that make successful people feel human immediately.

02THE BET

Put one defining decision on screen.

The guest reconstructs what they knew, what they risked and why they chose to move.

03THE NUMBER

One number that changed the business.

A first cheque, a loss, a target, an order, a headcount or a deal — used only when meaningful.

04THE HARD QUESTION

No generic answers.

One researched question about a failure, criticism, strategic error or difficult period.

05LEBANON / OUT

Stay, leave, expand — or do both?

A recurring lens on what Lebanon gives founders and what regional markets make possible.

06ONE CALL

The advice that fits in one phone call.

The guest speaks directly to someone starting today: one move to make, one trap to avoid.

10 / Why Elissa Fahd

The host is not the star of the interview. She is the reason the guest opens up.

The strongest host positioning is credible, prepared and curious — without trying to out-perform the person in the chair.

Elissa’s on-screen role

The audience’s intelligent proxy.

Ask what viewers would want to know if they had access to the room — then go one layer deeper with preparation and follow-up.

Warm enough for personal stories.
Direct enough for difficult answers.
PREPARATION

Know the timeline, numbers, difficult moments and public record before the guest sits down.

CLARITY

Translate business language into questions a broad television audience can follow.

LISTENING

Use the answer to find the next question rather than racing through a script.

COMPOSURE

Stay calm when the conversation becomes sensitive, surprising or emotionally personal.

IDENTITY

Develop a recognizable interview voice through recurring questions and disciplined tone.

CURIOSITY

Make the guest explain the “why” behind a decision, not only the result.

This positioning deliberately avoids unsupported biographical claims. The pitch presents the host role and the on-screen standard the format is designed to build around Elissa.

11 / Visual & production direction

A room that feels like access.

Premium but restrained: the visual language should signal confidence, depth and modern Beirut — without looking like a news desk or a luxury showroom.

THE BUILDERSصُنّاع
CAM 01
SET

Dark, architectural, warm

Charcoal and warm-black surfaces, restrained gold, textured materials and one strong visual signature.

CAMERA

Close enough to read silence

Two principal singles, a clean two-shot and slow movement only when it adds emotional weight.

LIGHT

Faces first

Soft sculpted key light, controlled background falloff and practical highlights rather than over-lit news brightness.

GRAPHICS

Minimal evidence

Use timelines, a key number, a deal, a quote or an archival image when it moves the story forward.

MUSIC

Modern restraint

A short, recognizable sonic identity with subtle transitions — no constant score under serious conversation.

WARDROBE

Editorial, not corporate

Clean, contemporary and neutral enough to keep attention on faces, stories and the conversation.

A new lane that still feels native to the channel.

Builds on interview strength

Al Jadeed already programs multiple conversation-led formats, so the proposed show uses a familiar editorial muscle in a new subject territory.

02

Broadens the definition of influence

Founders, investors, executives and business families become part of the channel’s wider conversation about Lebanon and the region.

03

Creates a premium sponsor environment

Business leadership naturally aligns with categories seeking professional, affluent and aspirational audiences.

04

A strong interview archive can continue generating discovery on YouTube, social video, search and audio long after air date.
